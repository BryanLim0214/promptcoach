## The specification, as implemented

**Rubric** - 6 dimensions, scale 1-4, 4 planning + 2 evaluative. `framework/rubric.py`

| Dimension | Kind | kappa (Proem pair) | within one |
|---|---|---|---|
| Context Clarity | planning | 0.851 | 100.0% |
| Specificity | planning | 0.967 | 100.0% |
| Iteration Awareness | evaluative | 0.841 | 90.0% |
| Audience Awareness | planning | 0.886 | 96.7% |
| Constraint Setting | planning | 0.916 | 100.0% |
| Reflection | evaluative | 0.645 | 80.0% |

> The two evaluative dimensions had the lowest agreement in both grader pairs, but which one was lowest changed: Reflection (kappa 0.645) in Proem and Iteration Awareness (kappa 0.404) in the replication. These are AI-grader statistics; they say nothing about students.

Overall ICC(2,2) 0.976. Overall weighted kappa is `n/a` in the source table, so none is computed here either.

**Taxonomy** - 7 categories. 3 detectable from the prompt alone; 1 needs the paired spoken record; 3 need session history. `framework/taxonomy.py`

| Failure | Visible in | Layer 5 checks |
|---|---|---|
| Under specification | prompt text | Whichever of the four planning dimensions the coaching question named |
| Social framing | prompt text | Context Clarity |
| Intent drift | paired spoken record | Any dimension present in the spoken record and absent from the prompt |
| Blind acceptance | session history | Iteration Awareness |
| Undiagnosed retry | session history | Iteration Awareness |
| Delegated judgment | prompt text | Reflection |
| Borrowed phrasing | session history | Context Clarity, scored on the words the student wrote herself |

**Ladder** - 4 levels, terminal at 4. `framework/ladder.py`

| Level | Gives | She still |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | One question on the top ranked failure | Diagnoses and revises unaided |
| 2 | Names the dimension at fault without saying what to add | Decides what to add and revises |
| 3 | Shows her own prompt with the weak dimension marked | Supplies the missing element |
| 4 | Worked contrast: two prompts differing on that dimension, with both outputs | States which is better and why, then revises her own |

> Scaffolding and authorship are separated by a line that is easy to cross without noticing: a scaffold that supplies the missing element has written the prompt, and the student has learned to recognize a good prompt rather than to write one. Guidance may narrow the search space and may not close it.

How help climbs (`policy/escalation.py`, `session.py`): a help cycle is the help given on one task for one top-ranked category. Help moves up one rung when the same category is ranked first on 2 consecutive attempts (first attempts and revisions alike) or when she asks; for the three diagnoses paired with an evaluative dimension, only when she asks or her text is unchanged. A revision is measured by Layer 5 first; the cycle stays open while the category persists and the paired dimension has not improved, and closes once level 4 has been issued. A first attempt and three revisions reach level 4 end to end in `test_review_r3.py`.

**Progression** - 5 bands on two separate scales, joined between 3 and 4 with no promotion rule across the join. `framework/progression.py`

| Band | Scale | Requires |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | planning dimension count | One planning dimension, named in the task |
| 2 | planning dimension count | Two planning dimensions, named |
| 3 | planning dimension count | Three or more, implied by a scenario and not named |
| 4 | evaluative output visibility | An evaluative dimension, with an output in front of her |
| 5 | evaluative output visibility | An evaluative dimension, with no output to look at |

> The two axes are not commensurable. Nothing in the construction says a band 4 task is harder than a band 3 task; it says band 4 demands a different kind of work. The ordering across the join is the weakest link in the progression.

**Sentinel** - 4 signals. `framework/sentinel.py`

> The sentinel separated the researchers' risk labels in both studies: AUC 0.81 in Proem (one run, 9 high-risk items) and 0.882 pooled over five runs in the replication, where single runs ranged from 0.825 to 0.929. The run-to-run range is larger than the gap between the two studies.

> Sentinel output goes to the student. It does not populate a teacher dashboard, generate an alert, or restrict what the student can do next.

Which detector runs: in a live session Layer 5 calls the judge engine's model-based detector (`GeminiEngine.sentinel`), the one `eval/benchmark.py` measured. The keyword rules (`DeterministicEngine.sentinel`) run only in the offline engine; they are an author-tested stand-in, and every Layer 5 result names its detector in `sentinel_source`. Layer 3 does not read the sentinel: it diagnoses with its own rules over the plan, the prompt and the session history. `test_review_r3.py`

**Reasoned revision** - `framework/revision.py`, switch in `policy/revision.py`

> This is a new version of the same prompt. Before it is scored, say in a sentence why you changed it.

F7: Requiring a reasoned revision produces no larger change between first and revised prompt scores than not requiring it.

**Commitments** - 4, each with a rejection test. `framework/commitments.py`

- **Content over fluency** - direct
- **Diagnostics belong to the student, and adaptation is visible to her** - argued
- **Accessibility in the default path** - transferred
- **Consent separate from use** - transferred

> Her predicted rating and the gap between it and the measured score belong to her. They never appear in a teacher view, aggregate or otherwise.

### What was chosen instead (34 choices)

Every value below is this implementation's, not the thesis's.

| Choice | Value | Borrowed from | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| `affect.explicit_signals_only` | `True` | none | Only what the student says counts - a request for help, or a statement that she is stuck. This gives up the responsiveness Build 2 had. |
| `affect.latency_inference` | `removed` | sec:build2 - Build 2 read long latency with errors as frustration and fast success as boredom. | Removed, not tuned. This implementation first shipped it disabled, because reading a pause as struggle 'would reduce difficulty for exactly the students who are doing the work'. Latency is still recorded with each attempt and drives nothing. |
| `calibration.show_gap_to_student` | `True` | none | Shown by default. The gap is a model score minus her guess, so the shared-priors threat applies to it, and confidence and prompt score are different constructs. Showing it may teach her to predict the scorer rather than to judge her prompt; F8 is the test of whether showing it helps at all. Off is the F8 control arm: the rating and the gap are still recorded, and the student is shown neither. |
| `calibration.teacher_view` | `never` | none | Not a toggle. The policy choice is to exclude calibration from even the aggregate teacher view, which is stricter than the commitment requires: a class-average gap is cheap to compute and invites ranking students by how well they guess a model's score. |
| `classifier.max_ranked` | `3` | none | Three keeps the overlap visible - sec:diagnose says under specification and social framing 'co occur constantly' - without turning the diagnosis into the report card sec:respond warns against. Respond still acts on the top one only. |
| `classifier.min_confidence` | `0.5` | none | Below this an observation is recorded as insufficient evidence rather than as a category. That distinction matters: 'the system could not tell' is not 'the student did not fail'. |
| `classifier.priority_order` | `['delegated_judgment', 'intent_drift', 'blind_acceptance', 'undiagnosed_retry', 'under_specification', 'borrowed_phrasing', 'social_framing']` | none | This ordering encodes a pedagogical judgment the thesis does not make: that delegating the decision is a worse failure than omitting a constraint. | `classifier.selection_mode` | `diagnosis` | none | Both modes are implemented so that experiment can actually be run. A system that only supported diagnosis-selected responses would make its own falsification impossible, which is why this is a switch and not a constant. |
| `confidence.clarification_floor` | `0.45` | none | Below this the system asks the student for something specific rather than diagnosing on weak evidence. Set too high it nags; set too low it diagnoses noise. Nothing in the thesis settles where it belongs. |
| `confidence.ensemble_temperatures` | `[0.2, 0.5, 0.8]` | sec:build1 - the PatternLens three judge ensemble at temperatures 0.2, 0.5, and 0.8, reporting mean and spread. ## Deliberately not implemented

**gamification** - Narrative game worlds depend on a wrong answer having a physical analogue, and it is not obvious what the physical consequence of an under specified prompt would be, so the framework does not adopt the gamification layer. Chapter 9 records that as a deliberate non transfer rather than an omission.

**mathematics error types** - The six mathematics error types are worthless outside mathematics. These are domain knowledge and do not transfer; the practice of classifying before responding does.

## Not implementable, and why

These are not omissions. Each needs something software cannot supply.

**Agreement with a human rater - Measure's third bar**

> sec:measure - "The third bar is agreement with a human rater, and this thesis does not clear it." It needs teachers scoring the same items. No amount of software closes it.

**Classroom feasibility of speech capture**

> sec:elicit - three untested assumptions: thirty students planning aloud in one room, transcription accuracy on middle school speech, and whether a twelve year old narrates candidly with peers within earshot.

**Whether the taxonomy covers authentic student prompts**

> F1. Needs a corpus of real middle school prompts and independent human annotators. No student data has been classified with it.

**Whether the band ordering holds**

> F3. Needs student performance across all five bands, and the two scales are not commensurable across the join between bands 3 and 4.

**Whether the worked contrast holds a student**

> F5. The framework's most exposed single claim. Needs a controlled comparison against a plain hint.

**Whether a reasoned revision, or a visible calibration gap, does anything**

> F7 and F8. The switches for both control arms exist here; the experiments need students.

**Any instructional effect whatsoever**

> sec:intro and sec:threats - no student has used the framework. The reference implementation has not been used with students.

---

**What running this shows.** That the specification is implementable: each layer's output is sufficient for the next layer's input, and the constraints the thesis states - including the reasoned revision, the calibration gap, a judge that is a different model from the generator, and scoring only what she composed - can be enforced in code rather than in prose.

**What it does not show.** Anything about students. It has not been used with students. The rules in policy/ are implementation choices, not findings, and some of them are refusals to act on signals the thesis says may be backwards.
